Abstract 

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) of agent har-nesses has emerged as an effective approach to enhancing the capabilities of large language model (LLM) agents. By searching the harness space for a specific LLM on a given dataset, RSI can discover a harness tailored to the ca-pabilities and behavioral characteristics of that model. However, such optimization often cre-ates a strong coupling between the learned har-ness and its target LLM, limiting generaliza-tion across models and requiring substantial computational resources to adapt to new LLMs. Moreover, existing RSI methods typically op-timize a harness using feedback from a sin-gle model and evaluate its generalization to other models only after training. This paradigm treats model differences primarily as a crite-rion for evaluating generalization rather than as valuable evidence for optimization. In this pa-per, we introduce a new perspective that treats models as data and places the harness at the center of optimization. Building on this per-spective, we propose M AD-RSI, a framework that directly optimizes a shared harness across multiple heterogeneous LLMs by incorporating their diverse failure trajectories into the train-ing process. Rather than assessing model differ-ences only after optimization, M AD-RSI lever-ages these differences as training evidence to guide the self-improvement of a shared harness. Furthermore, we introduce a new evaluation framework with two complementary metrics de-signed to assess the effectiveness of harness op-timization across models. Experiments across multiple datasets and LLMs demonstrate that MAD-RSI improves generalization over exist-ing approaches while reducing training costs. 

1 Introduction 

Large language model (LLM) agents have achieved strong performance on complex tasks, including reasoning and code generation (Yang et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025). Their effec-tiveness depends not only on the underlying LLM but also on the harness. Recent work on recursive self-improvement (RSI) has shown that iteratively searching and refining this harness can enhance agent capabilities without updating model parame-ters (Chan et al., 2026; Duan et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026). However, many RSI methods optimize a harness using execution feedback from a single tar-get LLM (Liu et al., 2026). Such specialization can entangle the learned harness with the capabilities and behavioral characteristics of that model, limit-ing its portability to other LLMs (Qu et al., 2026). Adapting the harness to a new model may therefore require another costly optimization run, making it difficult to build an ecosystem of reusable and continuously improving harnesses. This limitation also shapes how RSI is evaluated. A common protocol optimizes a harness with one LLM and assesses its transfer to other models (Lee et al., 2026; Xia et al., 2026). In this paradigm, dif-ferences among models primarily serve to measure generalization, rather than to guide improvement. Yet heterogeneous LLMs can exhibit different out-comes and execution trajectories on the same task under the same harness. A failure from one model, when contrasted with a success from another, may reveal deficiencies that are difficult to identify from either trajectory alone. These observations suggest a different question: Can behavioral differences among LLMs serve as training evidence for learn-ing a harness that generalizes across models? 

We address this question through Models-as-Data , a perspective that treats heterogeneous LLMs, observed under controlled inference configura-tions (DeepSeek-AI, 2026) (such as sampling), as sources of behavioral evidence while placing the shared harness at the center of optimization (Mehri et al., 2022). Instead of viewing models solely as separate optimization targets or subjects of subse-quent transfer evaluation, we use their successful and failed executions as complementary signals for harness improvement. Building on this perspective, we propose M AD-RSI, a framework for RSI of a shared harness us-ing evidence from multiple heterogeneous LLMs. MAD-RSI first organizes responses to shared queries across models and inference configurations into a structured tensor, preserving the conditions under which each outcome is observed. It then se-lects informative queries using both disagreement among models and coverage of distinct response patterns. Finally, it jointly diagnoses aligned suc-cessful and failed trajectories to inform subsequent harness revisions. In this way, behavioral differ-ences across models become inputs to the optimiza-tion process rather than observations reserved for evaluation. To assess RSI methods designed for multiple models, we further introduce an evaluation frame-work with two complementary measures of opti-mization effectiveness: success coverage , which captures whether at least one model succeeds on a task, and joint success , which captures whether all evaluated models succeed on that task. These popu-lation measures complement evaluations of transfer to previously unseen LLMs. We study M AD-RSI on legal reasoning, code generation, medical rea-soning, and agent safety tasks across heterogeneous LLMs. The results show improvements in perfor-mance across models and transfer in some settings, with tradeoffs that vary by task. On the general tasks, optimizing one shared harness also reduces cumulative training cost relative to running sepa-rate optimization processes for each development model. More broadly, this study invites a reconsid-eration of the role of LLMs in RSI: beyond being targets of harness optimization, they can provide the evidence that drives it. We summarize our con-tributions as follows: • Models-as-Data perspective. We formulate an RSI paradigm across models in which behavioral differences among heterogeneous LLMs become evidence for optimizing a shared harness, rather than serving only as a means of evaluating trans-fer after training. • MAD-RSI framework. We develop a shared harness optimization framework that combines structured response representations, evidence se-lection based on disagreement and behavioral coverage, and joint diagnosis of successful and failed trajectories. • Evaluation and empirical study. We introduce two complementary population measures for as-sessing harness optimization across models and evaluate generalization and training cost across diverse tasks and LLMs.

4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup Datasets. We use LawBench (Fei et al., 2024), MBPP+ (Liu et al., 2023), MedXpertQA (Zuo et al., 2025), and Agent-SafetyBench (Zhang et al., 2025) (SafetyBench). 

Models. For RSI, we employ DeepSeek-V4-Flash (DeepSeek-V4), DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3.8-Flash, and Qwen3.8-27B as the underlying target models. We assign equal weights to the four mod-els, with wm = 1 /4. To assess the generalization capability of the evolved harness, we further evalu-ate it on Doubao-Seed-2.0-Mini (Doubao-2.0) and GLM-5.3-Flash. 

Evaluation and Metrics. During M AD-RSI ev-idence collection, temperature is the sampling pa-rameter varied. We use T = 0 .0 and T = 1 .0, with two repetitions for each query–model–temperature combination. General-task experiments use three rounds, with two candidate slots per round. We re-port Pass@ M to measure coverage and Pass^ M to measure joint success. Main experiments use four models, while expanded evaluations include two additional unseen models. For general tasks, cor-rectness defines the pass event. For safety tasks, we report attack safety (AS), utility under attack (UA), and clean safety (CS). We also account for resource consumption alongside performance comparisons, reporting token usage and execution time. 

Baselines. We compare six configurations: the original harness, four single-executor baselines, and our M AD-RSI. We adopt RRSI (Xia et al., 2026) for general tasks and SHE (Qu et al., 2026) for safety tasks. Each baseline is optimized using a single executor model and evaluated across all four models. We select RRSI because it regular-izes both edit proposals and candidate selection to promote transfer to unseen benchmarks (Xia et al., 2026). This comparison examines whether cross-model evidence provides additional benefits within a framework already designed for generalization. For generalization evaluation, we select the base-line achieving the highest Pass^ 4 in the main ex-periments, breaking ties using Pass@ 4. For safety tasks, baseline selection is based on UA.

5 Related Work 

Existing methods for RSI of agent harnesses largely follow a common paradigm: optimizing a harness using a single execution model and subsequently evaluating its transfer across models (Hu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026; Xia et al., 2026). However, improvements achieved during optimization do not necessarily transfer to other models. Previous empirical findings suggest that harness modifications tailored to the failure modes of a source model may provide limited benefits to other models or even degrade their performance (Qu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Rakotonirina 5et al., 2023). This observation motivates us to in-corporate behavioral differences across models di-rectly into the training evidence. Unlike existing approaches, M AD-RSI compares the behaviors of different frozen execution models during evidence construction. Specifically, it aligns responses to the same query across models and sampling config-urations, leverages these aligned observations for query selection and joint diagnosis, and uses the resulting evidence to guide the optimization of a shared harness. 

6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduce Models-as-Data , a new perspective that treats heterogeneous LLMs as behavioral data sources for shared harness op-timization. Building on this perspective, we pro-pose M AD-RSI, which leverages behavioral dif-ferences across models to guide RSI. We further introduce two complementary metrics to evaluate optimization effectiveness across models. Experi-ments across diverse tasks and LLMs demonstrate improved generalization in several settings and re-duced cumulative training costs compared with in-dependent optimization on general tasks. We hope this work encourages a broader shift in RSI from optimizing harnesses for individual models toward learning reusable harnesses from diverse model behaviors. 

7 Limitations 

This work provides preliminary evidence for the feasibility and potential value of the Models-as-Data perspective, while more comprehensive ex-perimental validation is left for future work. This work is still in progress.
